User Manual

Welcome to Surface Explorer 4D, an advanced tool for visualizing and exploring parametric surfaces in 3 and 4 dimensions. This software allows mathematicians, students, and enthusiasts to manipulate complex geometries in real-time.

Version: 2.0
Graphics Engine: Qt RHI (Vulkan, Metal, Direct3D, OpenGL)
Developed by: Dioscorid

User Interface

The interface is organized into collapsible "Docks" (panels) located on the bottom status bar. Click a button once to open its corresponding dock, and click it again—or open a different dock—to close it.

  • Equations: Mathematical definition of the surface and its limits (Min/Max).
  • Renderer: Control visual modes (Solid, Wireframe), colors, and textures.
  • 3D Motion: Controls for classic rotation (Precession, Nutation, Spin).
  • 4D Motion: Controls for rotation in 4D space (Omega, Phi, Psi).
  • Script: Advanced editor for writing custom GLSL scripts for surfaces, textures, or procedural sounds.
  • Library: Manager for saved files (Surfaces, Textures, Sounds, and Records).

In the center is the interactive 3D Viewport (powered by Qt RHI).

Button States: With the exception of the Go/Stop button in the 3D Motion dock, all toggle buttons in the interface display the current active state of the setting, rather than the action that will occur when pressed.

The Library & Saving Your Work

The Library dock is the nerve center for managing your creations. It is divided into four main tabs, each designed to store a specific type of file. Understanding what is encapsulated in each category is crucial for organizing your presets in a modular way.

1. Surfaces

Saving a Surface captures the mathematical definition and the base visual style of your 3D/4D object. It is ideal for storing pure geometry without linking it to a specific animation or texture.

  • What it saves: The X, Y, Z, P equations (or the custom GLSL surface script), explicit constraints (U, V, W), mathematical constants (A-F, S), U/V/W limits, grid resolution (Steps), base colors (Surface and Border), and lighting/projection modes.
  • What it ignores: Textures, camera positions, 3D/4D paths, and animation speeds.

2. Textures

Saving a Texture isolates the visual pattern or image. It can be applied interchangeably to the object's surface or the background.

  • What it saves: The GLSL texture code (or the absolute path of the loaded image), the two active colors in the interface (Color 1 and Color 2), and 2D transformations applied via mouse or 2D View (Zoom, Pan X/Y, Rotation).
  • What it ignores: The 3D geometry it is mapped onto. You can safely load a saved texture onto any new surface.

3. Sounds

The Sounds tab stores audio configurations, which can be procedural mathematical synths or based on external files.

  • What it saves: Purely procedural audio scripts (using the //SYNTH: directive) or absolute paths to local audio files (//MUSIC: directive).
  • How to use it: By right-clicking a sound in the tree and selecting "Add to Current Script", the audio directive will be automatically injected into the active script, seamlessly merging the sound with your graphics.

4. Records (Complete Presets / Scenes)

A Record save is an absolute and complete snapshot of your entire workspace. It is the most powerful format, designed for animations and final renders.

  • What it saves: Absolutely everything. The surface geometry, applied textures (both surface and background), active sounds, all mathematical constants, colors, 4D lighting configuration, 3D/4D path tracking data, and all continuous rotation speeds (Spin, Nutation, Precession, Omega, Phi, Psi).
  • Camera and Observer: It perfectly immortalizes the exact position of the 3D camera (the zoom and orbital rotation you set with the mouse) and the distance of the 4D Observer (P+/P-). When you reopen a Record, the framing, audio, and animation will resume exactly as you left them.

Workflow & Organization

  • Smart Saving: By clicking the Save button in the Script dock, the program will automatically understand whether you are working on a Surface, a Texture, or a Sound based on the "Mode" button, and will prompt you with the correct format.
  • Context Menu: By right-clicking in an empty spot within the Library tree, you can use "Save ... Here" to save a new file directly into that specific folder. You can also copy, cut, paste, and delete items in bulk.
  • Drag & Drop: You can drag files and folders between the various branches to reorganize your collection; the file system will physically update in real-time.

Interaction & Navigation

Navigation controls change slightly depending on whether you are viewing a 3D/4D surface or previewing a 2D Texture/Image.

3D & 4D Surface Mode

  • Left Mouse Button (Drag): Rotates the surface (or the camera, depending on the mode selected in the UI).
  • Mouse Wheel: Zooms in and out.

2D Texture/Image Mode

When viewing the texture preview (Flat View):

  • Left Mouse Button (Drag): Rotates the texture continuously.
  • Mouse Wheel: Zooms into the texture.
  • Left Mouse Button (Single Click): Instantly rotates the image by 90 degrees.

Projection Modes

Surface Explorer 4D offers different projection methods to map geometry onto your screen. You can cycle through these modes using the dedicated button in the bottom status bar:

  • Perspective: Standard 3D perspective projection with depth perception.
  • Wide Perspective: A wider field of view (FOV) that creates a more dramatic, expansive rendering of large or complex surfaces.
  • Orthogonal: Flat, parallel projection without depth distortion. Ideal for technical visualization and exact proportional sizing.
Important: When using Perspective or Wide Perspective modes, if the surface moves too close to the camera (near-clipping plane), visual artifacts or "holes" may appear. If this happens, try switching the projection mode to Orthogonal.

Parametric Equations

The core of the software is the mathematical parser (based on ExprTk). Because the software generates 2-dimensional surfaces embedded in 3D or 4D space, the mathematical definitions must always ultimately reduce to exactly two independent parameters.

The 2-Parameter Rule (Three Methods)

You can achieve this reduction to two dimensions using one of the following three methods:

  1. Direct 2D Parametrization: Write the main spatial equations (X, Y, Z, P) directly as functions of exactly two lowercase parameters (u, v).
  2. Constraint Mode: Write the main equations using three lowercase parameters (u, v, w), but bind one of them to the other two by providing an explicit equation in the Constraints tab (e.g., w = f(u, v)).
  3. Composition Mode: Write the main equations using uppercase composite variables (U, V, W). Then, in the Composition tab, define how these uppercase variables are calculated from the base parameters u and v (e.g., U = cos(u)*sin(v)).
    Note: You cannot mix lowercase (u, v, w) and uppercase (U, V, W) spatial variables in the main coordinate equations.

Syntax

Variable Description
u, v, w Main lowercase parametric coordinates.
U, V, W Uppercase composite variables, defined in the Composition tab.
A, B, C... F Constants dynamically adjustable via sliders in the Equations panel.
s A dynamic variable controlled by its own slider for structural changes.
pi Constant π (3.14159...).
e Euler's number (2.71828...).

Resolution (Steps)

Within the Equations dock, you will find the Steps input field. This parameter controls the geometric resolution of the generated 3D mesh:

  • Higher values (e.g., 100-200): Generate a smoother, highly detailed, and perfectly curved surface, but require more processing power from your CPU/GPU.
  • Lower values (e.g., 10-50): Create a blocky, low-polygon mesh. This is excellent for boosting performance on older devices, exploring complex 4D shapes rapidly, or achieving a stylized, retro wireframe aesthetic.

The 4th Dimension

Surface Explorer calculates geometry in R4 (X, Y, Z, W) and projects it into R3 for visualization.

4D Rotations

In addition to classic 3D rotations, three hyperspatial rotations involving the W axis are available:

  • Omega (X-W): Rotates the X plane onto the W axis.
  • Phi (Y-W): Rotates the Y plane onto the W axis.
  • Psi (Z-W): Rotates the Z plane onto the W axis.

Lighting Modes (3D vs 4D)

To seamlessly switch between classic 3D lighting and advanced 4D lighting, simply change the active motion dock:

  • 3D Lighting (3D Motion Dock): When the 3D Motion dock is active, the engine uses standard 3D lighting. It ignores 4D depth, lighting the object as if it were a standard 3D shape and preserving classic shadows and volumes.
  • 4D Lighting (4D Motion Dock): When the 4D Motion dock is opened, hyperspatial lighting takes over. You can cycle through the different 4D lighting models by clicking the dedicated Lighting Button inside the dock:
    • Directional Lighting: Simulates a global "4D Sun" with parallel rays from infinity, providing even, studio-like illumination across the hyper-surface.
    • Observer: Simulates a "light" positioned at the 4D observation point, dynamically brightening or darkening the surface based on its proximity in the 4th dimension while preserving 3D volume.
    • Slice: Highlights the intersection with the current 3D observation hyperplane (useful for cross-sections) with soft volumetric transitions.

Rendering & Textures

You can choose between different visualization modes:

  • Basic: Flat color (fast).
  • Phong: Realistic specular lighting.
  • Wireframe: Displays the polygonal grid.
  • Background: Displays the 2D interactive environment behind the 3D objects.

Applying Textures (Surface vs. Background)

Textures (both procedural scripts and static images) can be applied in two distinct ways depending on your current rendering mode target:

  • Surface Textures: When the Basic or Phong modes are active, applying a texture from the Library (or activating the Texture checkbox) will wrap the selected image or script directly onto the geometry of the 3D/4D surface.
  • Background Textures: By selecting the Background rendering mode first, any texture you load or procedural script you run will be applied to the 2D background behind the surface. In this mode, both images and procedural effects remain fully interactive: you can pan, zoom, and rotate them using the mouse, just like you would with the 3D camera.

Note: If no background image is explicitly loaded, a fallback procedural Deep Space gradient will be automatically generated to ensure a sleek presentation.

Procedural Textures (GLSL)

Besides loading images (PNG/JPG), you can write custom GLSL scripts to generate mathematical textures in real-time.

// Example Texture Script
float r = sin(u * 10.0);
float g = cos(v * 10.0);
return vec3(r, g, 0.5);

Animation & Video

Real-Time Animations (t parameter)

The engine supports dynamic, real-time surface animations, allowing both internal geometric deformations and continuous spatial rotations.

Time-Based Morphological Animations

To animate a shape's geometry, simply include the t (time) variable within your parametric equations (e.g., X = sin(u + t)).

  • Automatic Detection: When the system detects the t variable in your formulas, the START button is automatically primed for animation.
  • START / STOP: Clicking START begins the flow of time (t increases fluidly), animating the geometry in real-time. The button will change its label to STOP. Clicking STOP freezes the animation instantly, maintaining the exact deformed shape of that moment.
  • Customizing Time Speed & Direction: There is no dedicated global speed slider for the t variable. To adjust the animation speed, simply multiply t by the s constant (e.g., X = sin(u + t * s)). Because the s slider in the Equations dock can take negative values, you can use it to speed up, slow down, and even reverse the direction of the movement dynamically!
  • Reset View: The Reset button does not rewind the internal animation. It only restores the camera's zoom and rotation to the center, allowing you to explore the moving or paused surface without losing its current morphological state.

3D & 4D Spatial Animations

Beyond internal shape morphing, you can animate the entire surface spatially using the dedicated controls in the 3D and 4D docks.

  • Continuous Rotations: You can set the surface to spin continuously by adjusting the specific speed sliders. The 3D dock controls standard spatial rotations (Spin, Precession, Nutation), while the 4D dock controls complex hyperspace rotations (Omega, Phi, Psi).
  • Path Following: In the 3D dock, you can activate the Path feature to automate the camera or the object's movement along the calculated parametric curves.
  • Independent Speed Controls: Unlike the morphological time t, all spatial rotations and path movements have their own dedicated speed sliders within their respective docks, giving you complete and independent control over the object's physical motion.

The software includes a high-quality offline recording system.

Recording Workflow

  1. Press REC.
  2. Set the video duration (in seconds).
  3. Choose where to save the MP4 file.
  4. The software will render frame by frame (eliminating stutter caused by PC lag).
  5. Upon completion, a smooth 30 FPS video will be generated using FFmpeg.
Technical Note: Offline rendering decouples real-time from video time. Even if calculating a frame takes 1 second, the final video will be perfectly fluid.

Script Dock & Advanced Scripting

For expert users, the Script dock provides a powerful GLSL code editor to bypass the standard equations and directly program the GPU for surfaces, textures, and sounds.

Script Buttons

The Script dock features several action buttons to compile and apply your code depending on your current goal:

  • Run Script: Compiles the code to define the 3D/4D geometry of a surface (bypassing the Equations panel). Special variables like u_min, steps, A, B can be declared at the top of the script to automatically configure the UI sliders.
  • Apply Texture Script: Applies the GLSL code as a procedural texture. It will be mapped onto the 3D surface or the 2D background, depending on which target is currently active in the Renderer dock.
  • Run Sound / Stop Sound: Parses the script for //SYNTH: or //MUSIC: directives and executes the audio engine. The button toggles to "Stop Sound" during playback and can be clicked again to halt the audio.
  • Save Script: Prompts you to save the current editor content as a new Surface, Texture, or Sound preset in your Library.

Sounds & Audio

Surface Explorer 4D features a dedicated audio engine that supports both physical audio tracks and procedural synthesizers, allowing you to combine breathtaking visuals with dynamic soundscapes.

Supported Formats

The library recognizes two types of audio elements within the Sounds tab:

  • Physical Audio (Music): Standard .mp3, .wav, and .ogg files. These are automatically labeled with an [AUDIO] tag in the Library tree so you can easily distinguish them.
  • Procedural Audio (Synth): .json files containing mathematical formulas or script code designed to generate sound waves dynamically using the internal synthesizer.

How to Use Audio in Scripts

Audio tracks and synths are directly integrated into your visual scripts. To apply a sound to your current visual creation:

  1. Open the Sounds tab in the Library panel.
  2. Single-click any audio file or procedural preset in the tree.
  3. The software will automatically clean up any previous audio directives, inject the new sound at the top of your current script Editor, and start playing it instantly!

Script Directives

Behind the scenes, clicking an item injects one of these special directives into your code:

  • //MUSIC: path/to/file.mp3 - Tells the media player to stream the background music track.
  • //SYNTH: formula - Feeds the mathematical formula to the internal synthesizer to generate procedural sounds.

Library & Presets

The library system allows you to organize your work:

  • Surfaces (*.json): Saves the essential geometric elements such as parametric equations, mathematical constants, limits, rotation and the lighting type. It always loads with default colors (green for the surface, dark gray for the background) and does not include any animation.
  • Textures (*.json): Saves texture scripts (GLSL) or image references.
  • Sounds (*.json, *.mp3, *.wav, *.ogg): Saves audio tracks and procedural synthesizer presets.
  • Records (*.json): Saves the surface in a complete composite form. This includes customized colors, applied textures, active sounds, and optionally entire animated scenes, including camera paths (3D/4D) and rotation speeds.

You can create folders, cut, paste, and delete items directly from the interface via the context menu (right-click).

Factory Presets

The application includes a built-in library of presets to help you get started. To ensure the software remains lightweight and fast to download, the media files within these presets are carefully managed:

  • Textures: The included texture presets consist primarily of procedural JSON scripts. While standard image files are perfectly supported and included, their number is strictly limited to avoid bloating the application's overall file size.
  • Sounds: Similarly, sound presets rely mostly on procedural audio scripts. A select few standard audio files (such as .wav) are included, but they are also kept to a minimum to conserve space and maintain high performance.
