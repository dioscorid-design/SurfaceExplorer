Library, Presets & Saving Your Work

← Script Dock & Advanced Scripting  ·  Contents  ·  Modular Activation & Master Controls →

The Library dock organizes your work into four tabs, each storing one kind of file.

 

1. Surfaces (*.json)

Saving a Surface captures the mathematical definition and the base visual style of your 3D/4D object. It is ideal for storing pure geometry without linking it to a specific animation or texture.

  • What it saves: the geometry (equations or surface script, constraints, constants A–F and S, U/V/W limits, Steps, 4D rotation state), the visual style (color, transparency, render mode, lighting) and the framing (camera, projection, field of view).
  • Multi-mesh surfaces: the appearance and the domain of every grid you customized are saved too, with the domain of the All scope and the active All/Mesh scope.
  • What it ignores: Textures, the background (both its color and any background texture), 3D/4D paths, and animation speeds. A Surface always loads over the default dark gray background; to store a scene complete with textures and background, save a Record instead.

 

2. Textures (*.json)

Saving a Texture isolates the visual pattern or image. It can be applied interchangeably to the object's surface or the background.

  • What it saves: The GLSL texture scripts or the absolute path of the loaded image, the two active colors in the interface (Color 1 and Color 2), and 2D transformations applied via mouse or 2D View (Zoom, Pan X/Y, Rotation).
  • What it ignores: The 3D geometry it is mapped onto. You can safely load a saved texture onto any new surface of the same mode: a Ray Marching texture loaded on a parametric surface (or the other way round) switches mode and brings up that mode's default surface. Images, and textures applied to the background, work in both.

 

3. Sounds (*.json, *.mp3, *.wav, *.ogg)

The Sounds tab stores audio configurations, which can be procedural mathematical synths or based on external files.

  • What it saves: Purely procedural audio scripts (using the //SYNTH: directive) or absolute paths to local audio files (//MUSIC: directive).
  • How to use it: click a sound in the tree to add its directive to the current script and play it.

 

4. Records / Motions (*.json)

A Record save is an absolute and complete snapshot of your entire workspace. It is the most powerful format, designed for animations and final renders.

  • What it saves: Absolutely everything. The surface geometry in a complete composite form, customized colors, applied textures (both surface and background), active sounds, all mathematical constants, 4D lighting configuration, 3D/4D path tracking data, and all continuous rotation speeds (Spin, Nutation, Precession, Omega, Phi, Psi).
  • Camera and Observer: when you reopen a Record, the framing and audio resume as you left them, while the animation restarts from the beginning.
  • Sync Focused Texture: Right-click the Record loaded in the scene and choose Sync Focused Texture to bring in the current code of the Library textures it uses — surface, background or both — while keeping the Record's colors, constants A–F and zoom, pan and rotation. It does not save: save the Record again to keep the result.

 

Workflow & Organization

  • Context Menu: Right-click an item or an empty spot in the Library tree (long-press on touch devices) to open a context menu with the operations on files and folders.
  • Drag & Drop: on desktop, files and folders can be dragged between branches to reorganize them on disk.
  • Unsaved work: before opening a surface or a record, pressing NEW, switching mode or closing the app, Surface Explorer asks whether to save if the scene differs from the one opened or last saved — putting a control back where it was does not count. Picking another texture or sound, or switching the texture off, asks only if that module itself was changed.
