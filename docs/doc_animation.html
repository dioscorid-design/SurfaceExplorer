Real-Time Animations (t_Motions)

The engine supports dynamic, real-time surface animations, allowing both internal geometric deformations and continuous spatial rotations.

 

Time-Based Morphological Animations

To animate a shape's geometry, simply include the t (time) variable within your parametric equations (e.g., X = sin(u + t)).

  • Customizing Time Speed & Direction: There is no dedicated global speed slider for the t variable. To adjust the animation speed, simply multiply t by the s constant (e.g., X = sin(u + t * s)). Because the s slider in the Equations dock can take negative values, you can use it to speed up, slow down, and even reverse the direction of the movement dynamically!

 

3D & 4D Spatial Animations

Beyond internal shape morphing, you can animate the entire surface spatially using the dedicated controls in the 3D and 4D docks.

  • Continuous Rotations: You can set the surface to spin continuously by adjusting the specific speed sliders. The 3D dock controls standard spatial rotations (Spin, Precession, Nutation), while the 4D dock controls complex hyperspace rotations (Omega, Phi, Psi).
  • Path Following: In the 3D dock, you can activate the Path feature to automate the camera or the object's movement along the calculated parametric curves. For fly-through paths that travel inside a surface, combine this with the Fov slider of the same panel: a wider lens angle keeps nearby walls from filling the screen (see Projection Modes).
  • Starting Over: a stopped motion resumes where it stopped (DEPARTURE continues the path from the point it had reached). To bring rotations, camera paths and the t animations back to where the scene opened, press RESET and choose Motions.

The software includes a high-quality offline recording system.

 

Recording Workflow

Press REC and choose Video or Image. For a video you then choose duration, frame rate, resolution and destination: the video is rendered offline, frame by frame, and assembled into an MP4.

Current View Size gives a video with the shape of the 3D view. The fixed resolutions (1080p, 2K, 4K) are 16:9: the Native Resolution engine redraws the scene for that frame with the same vertical field of view, so the sides show more or less than the screen; Screen Upscale scales the view as it is on screen and fills the rest with black bars.

Image renders the scene once, off screen, at the size you choose and saves it as a PNG, so the sharpness does not depend on your monitor. Pick the aspect ratio (current view, 16:9, 3:2, 4:3, 1:1, the portrait formats, the A series or your own) and the size in pixels, up to the largest side the GPU can render (16384 pixels on a recent Mac). On desktop and iPad you can instead give a print size in centimetres or inches and a resolution in dpi. While the window is open a frame in the 3D view shows what will be in the image: for a wider format the vertical field of view is reduced, so the image shows what the frame shows. The window estimates the render time; a heavy scene at a very large size can take long, and the system may stop a GPU job that lasts too long. Animations are frozen at the moment you press REC. Wireframe lines are one pixel wide, so at very large sizes they look thinner.

Technical Note: Offline rendering decouples real-time from video time. Even if calculating a frame takes 1 second, the final video will be perfectly fluid.
