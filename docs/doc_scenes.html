Scenes

← Library, Presets & Saving Your Work  ·  Contents  ·  Modular Activation & Master Controls →

A scene puts several surfaces together in the same space. Each surface is an object: it keeps its equations, colors, textures, motions and sound, and you place it, size it and move it independently of the others. Everything that is not an object belongs to the environment: the camera with its paths and rotations, the background, the environment sound and, in 4D, the observer and the 4D light. In a hyperbolic scene the environment is the space itself.

Version 2.0 offers three kinds of scene:

SceneSpaceWhat it can contain
3Dordinary spaceparametric Shapes without the P coordinate, Ray Marching Shapes, Cross Sections
4Dfour-dimensional spaceparametric Shapes, with or without the P coordinate
H³hyperbolic spaceparametric Shapes without the P coordinate, 3D Ray Marching Shapes

 

Starting a Scene

  1. Press NEW: a scene always starts empty.
  2. Press Scene on the status bar and choose 3D, 4D or H³.

If something is still on screen, Scene asks you to press NEW first. The status bar then shows the kind of scene, a selector with the object you are working on (for example 2/3 Torus); the T slider after REC moves the scene time (see Time below). To leave the scene, press NEW again.

 

Adding Objects

Right-click a Shape in the Shapes tab of the Library and choose Add to Scene (the item appears only once a scene has started). The object enters at the centre of the scene, at the size at which its Shape was framed: it looks as it does when you open it on its own.

Once a scene has started, a plain click on a Shape or a record replaces nothing: a notice reminds you to use Add to Scene. Records are not added: you add their Shape, then give it textures, motions or sound from the Library and the docks.

Not every Shape fits every space. When it does not, Add to Scene says why:

  • a 3D scene does not accept objects with the P coordinate (they need a 4D scene);
  • a 4D scene does not accept Ray Marching Shapes, Cross Sections included;
  • an H³ scene does not accept objects with the P coordinate, Cross Sections (a slice of a 4D shape has no meaning in hyperbolic space) or Shapes that are already pictures of hyperbolic space (saved as H³ Shapes, for example in the Poincaré ball).

 

Selecting: the Environment or an Object

The Objects dock lists the environment first, then the objects; the selector on the status bar opens the same list. What is selected decides what the commands act on.

With an object selected

  • The docks show and change that object: equations, colors, textures, rendering, sound.
  • The mouse and the fingers rotate, move and resize it (see Help > Mouse & Touch); in the 3D dock the arrows move it and Back/Forward change its size. The X, Y, Z fields follow.
  • GO and the rotation speeds rotate that object only.
  • The Path fields of the 3D and 4D docks become the object's path, added to its position (in a 4D scene P(t) moves it along the fourth axis). DEPARTURE applies the fields and starts or stops the path, and START starts from what the fields say. To remove the path, empty all the fields and press Enter. While what is written is not applied, the field has an amber border; Esc puts back the applied path.
  • RESET brings back its motions and its entry size.

With the environment selected

  • The equation fields are off: the environment has no surface. The constants A–F remain, for the camera paths. The texture goes on the background.
  • The 3D dock moves the camera; GO rotates the whole scene around its centre, objects and sky included.
  • The mouse and the fingers rotate, slide and zoom the whole scene, or reshape the camera path while it runs (see Help > Mouse & Touch and Animation & Video).
  • DRIVE flies the camera freely among the objects (see Interaction & Navigation): the environment stops, its path and its rotations, while the other objects keep their motions; T runs with the flight, so the objects enter and leave the scene at their times, and the T slider replays the flight. RESET brings the camera back to its start.
  • RESET acts on the whole scene: View brings back the opening framing, Motions brings every object and T back to the start.

START/STOP (the master button) always acts on the whole scene: every object, the camera path, the timeline and every sound.

 

Fixed Objects: a Surface as Scenery

The environment of a scene has no surface. When a surface must act as scenery, for example a torus inside which you build the scene, add it as an object, set it up while it is free (position, size, colors, texture) and then make it fixed with the Fixed (part of the environment) checkbox of the Objects dock. A fixed object is part of the environment for all practical purposes:

  • it cannot be modified: equations, constants, rendering, position and size are off in the docks; its texture and colors, however, stay its own and can be changed while it is fixed;
  • it cannot be removed: Remove is off (Duplicate stays: the copy is a normal object);
  • it is always in the scene: no In/Out window;
  • it has no motions of its own: fixing it stops GO and its paths, and it moves only with the world. The formulas of its paths stay written but do not run; once the object is free again, DEPARTURE starts them;
  • it answers gestures like the environment: they move the camera and turn or slide the whole scene.

In the list it appears as Torus (fixed). Unchecking the box makes it a normal object again, to modify or remove.

For example, an H³ scene can put the camera inside the tube of a fixed torus, with the actual objects living in the tube (a spinning sphere, a knot, a bead on a path). Projection shows the same scene from outside, in the Poincaré ball.

 

Around an Object: Reference Frames

The coordinates of a path or of a position are read from the centre of the scene or, if you choose, from the centre of an object, turned as it is turned.

  • Camera path around (Objects dock): where the camera's 3D path is read from. A circle of radius 2.4 around a torus stays on the core of its tube wherever the torus is.
  • Placed around (Objects dock, with an object selected): where its X, Y, Z position and its path are read from. A bead running in the tube of a torus stays in the tube.

The reference can be any object, fixed or moving. Whatever sits around it follows its position, path included, and its rotation, GO included: a moon around an earth running on its path follows it, and a satellite placed around the moon, with no path of its own, turns with it. Chains are allowed (the satellite around the moon around the earth); choices that would close a loop are not offered. A fixed object can also sit around a moving one (a ring around a planet). The camera follows a moving reference while its path runs.

In the dock list: Moon around Earth. When the reference is removed, whatever sat around it is read from the centre of the scene again.

A typical use: the camera riding inside the tube of a torus, fixed or travelling and turning, while other objects move around it or in its hole.

Time: T and the Windows

T is the time of the motion of the environment's camera: its path, its DRIVE flight or, without either, its rotations. It runs and stops with that motion, and RESET Motions brings it back to 0.

The In and Out fields make an object appear and disappear: outside its window the object is not drawn and its motions wait, so it enters exactly as it was at the start. The T slider on the status bar takes the camera's motion to any time, also after STOP to rewind it; it is off when the environment has no path, no flight and no rotations, and during REC. Objects have clocks of their own: the slider does not move their rotations, animations and paths.

 

Sound and Transparency

Each object can have its own sound, and they all play together. Run Sound / Stop Sound in the Script dock act on the selected object; START/STOP on all of them. In a video the sounds are mixed.

Transparent objects are drawn in the right order pixel by pixel, even when they intersect or pass through each other (for example two linked tori).

 

Saving and Opening Scenes

Scenes live in the Scenes tab of the Library. Right-click there and choose Save New Scene…, Save Scene Here… on a folder, or Save Scene As… on an existing scene. The file keeps the whole scene: its kind, the environment, every object complete with its placement, and the lattice. A click on a scene in the Scenes tab opens it.

 

Switching Space: 3D and H³

A started scene can move between flat and hyperbolic space with its objects inside. Click the button that shows the kind of scene on the status bar (3D, 4D or H³) and choose the other space:

  • positions, sizes and paths of the objects stay the same in scene units;
  • the camera keeps its point of view, and so does the opening view that RESET View brings back; a running camera path goes on;
  • the curvature radius is kept for when you return to H³.

The switch to H³ is refused, with the reason, when the scene holds a Cross Section or a Shape defined in hyperbolic space. A 4D scene stays 4D.

 

Hyperbolic Scenes (H³)

An H³ scene shows hyperbolic space as someone living inside it would see it.

  • The camera is a point of the space. Zooming and the 3D dock move it along the line it looks along (a geodesic); rotating by hand turns the world around the centre of the scene, sky included. Camera paths work as in 3D.
  • Objects are their usual 3D drawing carried into hyperbolic space from their centre: distances from the centre are kept, while the space opens up around them.
  • Curvature radius (Objects dock, from 0.5 to 50 scene units, 2 by default): the smaller it is, the more the geometry shows; very large, the space is almost Euclidean. Objects and camera stay where they are and as large as they are in scene units.
  • The sky is always the boundary at infinity of H³, drawn as a Poincaré ball: the choice of background shape is off.
  • Lattice {4,3,5}: the walls of hyperbolic space, a regular tiling by cubes, five of them around every edge (four in ordinary space). In its panel of the Objects dock the checkbox turns it on, Density says how far it reaches, Thickness and Color set its tubes.
  • Projection: Perspective is the view from inside. Orthogonal is the view from outside: the Poincaré ball model in front of you, with the lattice almost up to the boundary.
  • The 4D dock is off: a hyperbolic scene is a three-dimensional space.

 

The Lattice in 3D Scenes

A 3D scene has the flat twin of the hyperbolic lattice, Lattice {4,3,4}: cubes with four of them around every edge, which turn with the scene and fade out towards the edge of the piece that is drawn. Its panel in the Objects dock has the same controls as in H³, plus Size, the edge of the cubes in scene units (from 0.25 to 25). Density says how far it reaches from the cube around the camera.

 

Videos and Images of a Scene

REC records the scene as you see it, every object with its motions and its sounds. Image captures it at any size; the frame shown in the view can be dragged to choose the framing (see Animation & Video).
