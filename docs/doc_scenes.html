Scenes

← Library, Presets & Saving Your Work  ·  Contents  ·  Modular Activation & Master Controls →

A scene puts several surfaces together in the same space. Each surface is an object: it keeps its equations, colors, textures, motions and sound, and you place it, size it and move it independently of the others. Everything that is not an object belongs to the environment: the camera with its paths and rotations, the background, the environment sound and, in 4D, the observer and the 4D light. In a hyperbolic scene the environment is the space itself.

Version 2.0 offers three kinds of scene:

SceneSpaceWhat it can contain
3Dordinary spaceparametric Shapes without the P coordinate, Ray Marching Shapes, Cross Sections
4Dfour-dimensional spaceparametric Shapes, with or without the P coordinate
H³hyperbolic spaceparametric Shapes without the P coordinate, 3D Ray Marching Shapes

 

Starting a Scene

  1. Press NEW: a scene always starts empty.
  2. Press Scene on the status bar and choose 3D, 4D or H³.

If something is still on screen, Scene asks you to press NEW first. The status bar then shows the kind of scene, a selector with the object you are working on (for example 2/3 Torus); the T slider after REC moves the scene time (see Time below). To leave the scene, press NEW again.

In a scene that is still empty, Projection works when there is something to project: a background texture or, in H³, the lattice. With nothing, it says there is nothing to project.

 

Adding Objects

Right-click a Shape in the Shapes tab of the Library and choose Add to Scene (the item appears only once a scene has started). The object enters at the centre of the scene, at the size at which its Shape was framed: it looks as it does when you open it on its own. Its message (which sliders it uses) appears once, when it enters.

Once a scene has started, a plain click on a Shape or a record replaces nothing: a notice reminds you to use Add to Scene. Records are not added: you add their Shape, then give it textures, motions or sound from the Library and the docks.

Not every Shape fits every space. When it does not, Add to Scene says why:

  • a 3D scene does not accept objects with the P coordinate (they need a 4D scene);
  • a 4D scene does not accept Ray Marching Shapes, Cross Sections included;
  • an H³ scene does not accept objects with the P coordinate, Cross Sections (a slice of a 4D shape has no meaning in hyperbolic space) or Shapes that are already pictures of hyperbolic space (saved as H³ Shapes, for example in the Poincaré ball).

 

Selecting: the Environment or an Object

The Objects dock lists the environment first, then the objects; the selector on the status bar opens the same list. What is selected decides what the commands act on.

With an object selected

  • The docks show and change that object: equations, colors, textures, rendering, sound.
  • The mouse wheel, the pinch and Back/Forward change its size (one step = 10%).
  • The arrows of the 3D dock move it as you see it on screen, and so does dragging it with the right mouse button, or with two fingers on iPhone and iPad (the pinch still changes its size): its centre stays under the pointer or between the fingers, at the same depth (in H³ at the same distance from the camera), and the X, Y, Z fields follow. Dragging with the left button rotates it.
  • GO and the rotation speeds rotate that object only.
  • The Path fields of the 3D and 4D docks become the object's path: X(t), Y(t), Z(t) are added to its position, with their Speed; in a 4D scene P(t) moves it along the fourth axis. The formulas can use the constants A–F and s. DEPARTURE applies the fields and starts the path, and stops it while it runs; with the path stopped, a change waits for DEPARTURE. Empty all the fields and press Enter to remove the path, running or stopped: the object goes back to its X, Y, Z position. START starts from what is written in the fields of the selected object, as it does for the camera path.
  • RESET brings back its motions and its entry size.

With the environment selected

  • The equation fields are off: the environment has no surface. The constants A–F remain, for the camera paths. The texture goes on the background.
  • The wheel, the pinch and the 3D dock move the camera.
  • When no camera path is running, dragging and GO rotate the whole scene around its centre, objects included, and with them a background shaped as Sphere, Cylinder, Cube or Poincaré Ball (a Fixed background stays glued to the screen). Dragging with the right mouse button (on iPhone and iPad, moving two fingers together) slides the whole scene sideways: the camera moves without turning, and the centre of the scene stays under the pointer.
  • The background does not slide with it: a Fixed background is glued to the screen, and a sky at infinity does not move when the camera moves sideways. So it looks as if only the objects had moved, while the centre of the scene went with them: dragging keeps rotating the scene around that centre, wherever it now is on screen.
  • While a camera path runs, the mouse reshapes the path itself, and the change is written into its fields; with the path stopped the MOUSE button under DEPARTURE chooses between the scene and the path (see Animation & Video). Only the environment's path can be reshaped this way.
  • DRIVE flies the camera freely among the objects (see Interaction & Navigation): the environment stops, its path and its rotations, while the other objects keep their motions; T runs with the flight, so the objects enter and leave the scene at their times, and the T slider replays the flight. RESET brings the camera back to its start.
  • RESET acts on the whole scene: View brings back the opening framing (zoom included; the field of view stays), Motions puts every object back in its opening orientation, restarts its clocks and brings T back to 0.

START/STOP (the master button) always acts on the whole scene: every object, the camera path, the timeline and every sound.

 

Fixed Objects: a Surface as Scenery

The environment of a scene has no surface. When a surface must act as scenery, for example a torus inside which you build the scene, add it as an object, set it up while it is free (position, size, colors, texture) and then make it fixed with the Fixed (part of the environment) checkbox of the Objects dock. A fixed object is part of the environment for all practical purposes:

  • it cannot be modified: equations, constants, rendering, position and size are off in the docks; its texture and colors, however, stay its own and can be changed while it is fixed;
  • it cannot be removed: Remove is off (Duplicate stays: the copy is a normal object);
  • it is always in the scene: no In/Out window;
  • it has no motions of its own: fixing it stops GO and its paths and sets the speeds to zero; from then on it moves only with the world, with the rotations and paths of the environment;
  • it answers gestures like the environment: wheel, pinch and 3D dock move the camera, dragging turns the whole scene (in H³ it orbits the camera around the centre), dragging with the right button or with two fingers slides the whole scene.

In the list it appears as Torus (fixed). Unchecking the box makes it a normal object again, to modify or remove. The scene file remembers it (key fixed).

A practical consequence: with a fixed object selected, the Path fields of the 3D and 4D docks are its own, and they are off. The camera path is written with the environment selected (1/5 Environment in the selector), and fixing or freeing an object does not touch it. A path written on a free object moves the object, not the camera; fixing the object stops it.

For example, an H³ scene can put the camera inside the tube of a fixed torus, with the actual objects living in the tube (a spinning sphere, a knot, a bead on a path). Projection shows the same scene from outside, in the Poincaré ball.

 

Around an Object: Reference Frames

The coordinates of a path or of a position are read from a point. Usually it is the centre of the scene; you can choose the centre of an object instead, and then the coordinates are read from it, turned as it is turned. In Euclidean space this is a convenience; in H³ it is necessary, because there the chart that reads the coordinates has a base point, and “c + circle” read from the centre of the scene is not a circle around c. Read from the object, a circle stays a circle around it.

  • Camera path around (Objects dock): where the camera's 3D path X(t), Y(t), Z(t) is read from, together with its target. A circle of radius 2.4 around a torus stays on the core of its tube wherever the torus is and however it is turned. Only the 3D path: the 4D one is always read from the centre.
  • Placed around (Objects dock, with an object selected): where its X, Y, Z position and its path are read from. A bead running in the tube of a torus stays in the tube.

The reference can be any object, fixed or moving. Whatever sits around it follows its position, path included, and its rotation, GO included: a moon around an earth running on its path follows it, and a satellite placed around the moon, with no path of its own, turns with it. Chains are allowed (the satellite around the moon around the earth); choices that would close a loop are not offered. A fixed object can also sit around a moving one: it has no motions of its own, but it follows its reference (a ring around a planet). The camera follows a moving reference while its path runs, exactly at every frame, videos included; with the path stopped it stays where it is.

The scene file remembers them by name (cameraPathFrame of the scene, frame of the object). When the reference is removed, whatever sat around it is read from the centre of the scene again. In the dock list: Moon around Earth. Duplicate does not copy the reference, as it does not copy position and path. In a 4D scene the reference carries only the 3D part of the pose (3D position and rotation).

A typical use: the camera riding inside the tube of a torus, fixed or travelling and turning, while other objects move around it or in its hole.

In an H³ scene the dock also shows Curvature radius and the Lattice {4,3,5} panel; in a 3D scene, the Lattice {4,3,4} panel (see below).

 

Time: T and the Windows

T is the time of the motion of the environment's camera: its path, its DRIVE flight or, without either, its rotations. It runs and stops with that motion, and RESET Motions brings it back to 0.

The In and Out fields make an object appear and disappear: outside its window the object is not drawn and its motions wait, so it enters exactly as it was at the start. The T slider on the status bar takes the camera's motion to any time, also after STOP to rewind it; it is off when the environment has no path, no flight and no rotations, and during REC. Objects have clocks of their own: the slider does not move their rotations, animations and paths.

 

Sound and Transparency

Each object can have its own sound, and they all play together. Run Sound / Stop Sound in the Script dock act on the selected object; START/STOP on all of them. In a video the sounds are mixed.

Transparent objects are drawn in the right order pixel by pixel, even when they intersect or pass through each other (for example two linked tori), up to six layers on a computer (fewer on phones and tablets). In a very large image the Capture Image window warns when this is not possible, and the objects are then sorted one by one.

 

Saving and Opening Scenes

Scenes live in the Scenes tab of the Library. Right-click there and choose Save New Scene…, Save Scene Here… on a folder, or Save Scene As… on an existing scene. A scene file keeps:

  • the kind of scene (3D, 4D or H³) and the environment;
  • every object as a complete record (equations, textures, motions, sound), with its name, position, size, time window, path and, if it is fixed, the fact that it is fixed;
  • the rotation the environment gave to the whole scene, and the level at which Cross Sections are cut;
  • the lattice (on or off, density, thickness, color; in 3D also its size);
  • in H³, the curvature radius and the camera.

A click on a scene in the Scenes tab opens it. Its message gathers the messages of the environment and of every object, each one only once.

 

Switching Space: 3D and H³

A started scene can move between flat and hyperbolic space with its objects inside. Click the button that shows the kind of scene on the status bar (3D, 4D or H³) and choose the other space:

  • positions, sizes and paths of the objects stay the same in scene units;
  • the camera keeps its point of view, and so does the opening view that RESET View brings back; a running camera path goes on;
  • the H³ settings (curvature radius) are kept aside for the scene, and come back when you return to H³; the lattice settings belong to the scene and travel with it.

The switch to H³ is refused, with the reason, when the scene holds a Cross Section or a Shape defined in hyperbolic space, or when its environment has a surface of its own (scenes built before scenes started empty). A 4D scene stays 4D: its objects live in four dimensions.

 

Hyperbolic Scenes (H³)

An H³ scene shows hyperbolic space as someone living inside it would see it.

  • The camera is a point of the space. Wheel, pinch and 3D dock move it along the line it looks along (a geodesic); dragging turns the world around the centre of the scene, sky included. Dragging with the right button, or moving two fingers together, slides the view: the camera moves sideways in its own frame and the centre of the scene stays under the pointer; in the view from outside the camera slides inside the Poincaré ball, which keeps its size. The camera stays within 6 radii of curvature of the centre. Camera paths work as in 3D: their positions are read as vectors from the centre.
  • Objects are their usual 3D drawing carried into hyperbolic space from their centre: distances from the centre are kept, while the space opens up around them. Parametric Shapes and 3D Ray Marching Shapes share the same scene and hide each other correctly. Tubes have the same thickness everywhere.
  • Curvature radius (Objects dock, from 0.5 to 50 scene units, 2 by default): the smaller it is, the more the geometry shows; very large, the space is almost Euclidean. Objects and camera stay where they are and as large as they are in scene units.
  • The sky is always the boundary at infinity of H³, drawn as a Poincaré ball: the choice of background shape is off.
  • Lattice {4,3,5}: the walls of hyperbolic space, a regular tiling by cubes with dihedral angles of 72 degrees, so that five of them meet around every edge (four in ordinary space). The size of the cells belongs to H³: every edge is 1.62 radii of curvature long, whatever the radius. In its panel of the Objects dock the checkbox turns it on, Density says how far it reaches, Thickness is the thickness of the tubes in scene units (changing the curvature radius changes the cells, not the tubes) and Color the color.
  • Projection: Perspective is the view from inside. Orthogonal is the view from outside: the Poincaré ball model in front of you, with the lattice almost up to the boundary. The button works with the lattice alone, without objects.
  • Size: the zoom of an object stops where the computer would no longer represent it accurately (12 times the entry size with the default radius).
  • The 4D dock is off: a hyperbolic scene is a three-dimensional space.

 

The Lattice in 3D Scenes

A 3D scene has the flat twin of the hyperbolic lattice, Lattice {4,3,4}: cubes with four of them around every edge, which turn with the scene and fade out towards the edge of the piece that is drawn. Its panel in the Objects dock has the same controls as in H³, plus Size, the edge of the cubes in scene units (from 0.25 to 25). Density says how far it reaches from the cube around the camera. When a scene switches from H³ to 3D, Size is set so that the corners of the central cube stay where they were.

 

Videos and Images of a Scene

REC records the scene as you see it, every object with its motions and its sounds. Image captures it at any size; the frame shown in the view can be dragged to choose the framing (see Animation & Video).
