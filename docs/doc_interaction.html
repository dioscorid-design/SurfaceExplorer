Navigation controls change slightly depending on whether you are viewing a 3D/4D surface or previewing a 2D Texture/Image.

 

3D & 4D Surface Mode

  • Left Mouse Button (Drag): Rotates the surface (or the camera, depending on the mode selected in the UI).
  • Right Mouse Button (Drag): Slides the view sideways: the camera moves without turning, and the centre of the scene stays under the pointer.
  • Mouse Wheel: Zooms in and out.
  • On iPhone and iPad: one finger rotates like the left button, the pinch zooms like the wheel, and moving two fingers together slides the view like the right button.
  • While a camera path runs the mouse reshapes the path (see Animation & Video); in a scene it acts on the selected object or on the whole scene (see Scenes).
  • With DRIVE on: the keyboard, the mouse, the fingers and the game controller fly the camera instead; see Free Flight below.

 

Free Flight (DRIVE)

The DRIVE button on the status bar, next to Perspective, turns the camera into a vehicle that flies freely through the scene: with a single surface or among the objects of a scene, in 3D, 4D and H³. It moves with six degrees of freedom — forward and back, up and down, nose up and down, turning and rolling, even upside down — and in 4D it also travels toward the fourth coordinate W. It has inertia, so it speeds up, slows down and turns smoothly, and it goes through surfaces: you can fly inside a torus. A small panel at the bottom left of the view shows the speed and the controls; it does not appear in videos. STOP DRIVE (the same button), Esc or the master STOP end the flight.

  • The mouse is a stick: press the left button and move away from that point; the farther you go, the faster the camera turns, and releasing the button stops the turn. The right button does the same for the roll.
  • On iPhone and iPad the sticks appear where you put your thumbs: the left half of the view sets the speed and turns, the right half pitches and rolls; the round buttons on the right edge go up and down, and toward W where the 4D allows it. With a keyboard attached the keys work too.
  • With a game controller the sticks work like the thumbs on the touch screen: the left stick sets the speed and turns, the right stick pitches and rolls, and the triggers go up (R2) and down (L2), faster the further you push. A stick at rest does nothing and does not interrupt a recorded flight. The buttons are in the table; ○ stops DRIVE like Esc, and △ turns DRIVE on and off, also from outside it: in this version △ does not change the projection.
  • Large or small scenes: − and + halve or double the speeds (not the turns); the panel shows the scale.
  • The flight is recorded on the scene time T while you fly. RESET brings the camera back to the start of the flight, with T at 0; the T slider on the status bar moves along it; REC records it exactly as you flew it. While a recorded flight replays, the panel says REPLAY: touching any control takes over, and the flight is rewritten from that moment. With DRIVE off the flight stays, like a stopped camera path.
  • Saving: Save Record and Save Scene store the flight; opening the record or the scene replays it by itself.
  • One camera motion at a time: DRIVE stops the camera path and the rotations of the surface (in a scene, those of the environment: the other objects keep moving). A DEPARTURE, the GO of the environment, opening another preset, NEW and switching the space of a scene end the free flight, and its recorded flight with it.

 

2D Texture/Image Mode

When viewing the texture preview (Flat View):

  • Left Mouse Button (Drag): Rotates the texture continuously.
  • Right Mouse Button (Drag): Moves the texture, which follows the pointer at any zoom and rotation. This works on the surface, the background and the border texture.
  • On iPhone and iPad: one finger rotates the texture, a tap turns it by 90 degrees, the pinch zooms, and moving two fingers together moves the texture.
  • Mouse Wheel: Zooms into the texture.
  • Left Mouse Button (Single Click): Instantly rotates the image by 90 degrees.

 

Game Controller

On Mac, iPad and iPhone you can use a game controller, such as a PlayStation DualSense or DualShock 4, an Xbox controller, a Switch Pro Controller or an MFi controller. Pair it in the system settings (Bluetooth) or connect it with a cable. The status bar says when it connects or disconnects. The controller does what the mouse does, everywhere described above:

  • Left stick: like dragging with the left button. It rotates the surface or the selected object, and in Flat View it rotates the texture. While a camera path runs it turns the path.
  • Right stick: like dragging with the right button. It slides the scene, moves the selected object or moves the texture in Flat View. While a camera path runs it moves the path.
  • R2 and L2 triggers: like the mouse wheel. R2 zooms in and L2 zooms out, faster the harder you press. With an object selected they change its size.

The buttons press the buttons on the screen, under the same conditions (the names are those of the PlayStation controller; on an Xbox controller ✕ is A, ○ is B, □ is X and △ is Y):
