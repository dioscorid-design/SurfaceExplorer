The mouse, the fingers on iPhone and iPad, and a game controller on Mac, iPad and iPhone move the view, the objects of a scene and the camera paths, and fly the camera in DRIVE. What every gesture, key and button does is shown with pictures in two windows of the Help menu (on iPhone, iPad and Android, in the menu of the button at the top left):

  • Mouse & Touch: dragging, the wheel and the fingers, in the 3D and 4D view (surface, environment, selected object, camera path) and in the 2D texture view; then the keys, the mouse and the fingers of the free flight (DRIVE).
  • Game Controller: the sticks, the triggers and the buttons, also in the free flight. Pair the controller in the system settings (Bluetooth) or connect it with a cable; the status bar says when it connects or disconnects.

On a computer the windows can stay open beside the view while you try the commands.

 

Free Flight (DRIVE)

The DRIVE button on the status bar, next to Perspective, turns the camera into a vehicle that flies freely through the scene: with a single surface or among the objects of a scene, in 3D, 4D and H³. It moves with six degrees of freedom — forward and back, up and down, nose up and down, turning and rolling, even upside down — and in 4D it also travels toward the fourth coordinate W. It has inertia, so it speeds up, slows down and turns smoothly. It does not go through the surfaces — parametric, ray-marched, tubes and geodesic flows, in 3D, 4D and H³: when it touches one, it slides along it, an object moving into it pushes it away, and it passes through the holes of a cutout. The C key turns the collisions off, to fly inside a torus, and on again; on a game controller it is □, on iPhone and iPad the top round button on the right. The panel then says collisions off. A small panel at the bottom left of the view shows the speed and the controls; it does not appear in videos. STOP DRIVE (the same button), Esc or the master STOP end the flight.

  • The commands of the flight — keyboard, mouse, touch and game controller — are in Help > Mouse & Touch and Help > Game Controller. In large or small scenes all the speeds (not the turns) can be halved or doubled; the panel shows the scale.
  • The flight is recorded on the scene time T while you fly. RESET brings the camera back to the start of the flight, with T at 0; the T slider on the status bar moves along it; REC records it exactly as you flew it. While a recorded flight replays, the panel says REPLAY: touching any control takes over, and the flight is rewritten from that moment. With DRIVE off the flight stays, like a stopped camera path.
  • Saving: Save Record and Save Scene store the flight; opening the record or the scene replays it by itself.
  • One camera motion at a time: DRIVE stops the camera path and the rotations of the surface (in a scene, those of the environment: the other objects keep moving). A DEPARTURE, the GO of the environment, opening another preset, NEW and switching the space of a scene end the free flight, and its recorded flight with it.
